Create a runtime production sprite sheet for a 2D mobile pixel-art game.

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: top-down creature walk sprite atlas
Visual references: use the two images just shown as style and runtime-scale references—the existing midnight snack food atlas locks the chunky pixel density, dark brown-black outline, compact shading, and warm low-light palette; the simulator screenshot locks the 32x32 board-cell scale and top-down kitchen context.

Canvas and grid:
- Perfectly flat solid #FF00FF magenta background, uniform with no gradients, shadows, texture, or lighting variation.
- Exactly 4 rows by 2 columns of eight equal invisible cells.
- No visible grid, borders, separators, labels, text, UI, watermark, floor, cast shadow, or detached effects.
- Row order: row 1 mouse facing down, row 2 facing left, row 3 facing right, row 4 facing up.
- Two coherent walking frames per direction: frame 1 left paws forward, frame 2 right paws forward with a tiny tail sway.

Subject:
- The exact same tiny house mouse in all eight cells.
- Top-down slightly three-quarter readable game view, full body visible.
- Dusty gray-brown chunky body, oversized round muted-pink ears, pointed snout, thin curled tail, tiny black eyes with a mildly blank dopey expression.
- Slightly awkward and cute, not polished or glossy.
- Strong compact silhouette and dark brown-black 1-cluster outline matching the reference food sprites.
- Restrained 3-to-5 color shading, deliberate chunky pixels, no antialiasing, no dithering noise, no fur microtexture.
- Subject should occupy about 58% to 64% of each cell, stay centered, and remain the same camera distance and anatomical scale in every frame.
- Entire ears, paws, snout, and tail must fit fully inside each cell with generous magenta padding on all four sides; nothing may touch or cross any cell edge.
- The tail must stay tucked close to the body so it is never clipped.
- No food, snake, cheese, accessories, clothing, scenery, or extra animals.
